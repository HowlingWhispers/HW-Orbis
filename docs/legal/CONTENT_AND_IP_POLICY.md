# Content and Intellectual Property Policy

Status: legal draft for review
Applies to: content uploaded, authored, shared or published through The Howling Whispers
Last updated: 6 September 2026

## Plain-language summary

You keep responsibility for what you upload. Using someone else's work without permission can create legal problems for you and for the service.

Sharing a character or world for roleplay does not automatically give other users ownership of it.

## 1. User-owned content

Users retain whatever rights they lawfully hold in content they create and upload, subject to the licenses they grant to operate and display the service.

By uploading or publishing content, the user confirms that they have the rights or permissions reasonably necessary to do so.

## 2. License needed to operate the service

A user who uploads content grants The Howling Whispers a non-exclusive license, limited to what is reasonably necessary to:

- store the content;
- display it according to the user's visibility settings;
- transmit it to selected AI providers where required for roleplay;
- create technical copies and backups;
- process it for search, indexing, formatting and compatibility;
- make it available to other users where the owner deliberately marked it public or shared; and
- operate, secure and maintain the service.

This operational license does not transfer ownership to The Howling Whispers.

## 3. Public and shared assets

Marking an asset public or shared permits use only within the scope presented by the service.

Unless the owner provides a broader license, other users do not automatically receive the right to:

- claim ownership;
- sell the asset;
- redistribute the source asset outside the service;
- remove attribution or ownership information;
- make unauthorized derivative works; or
- upload it elsewhere as their own.

Roleplay access and ownership are different rights.

## 4. AI-generated material

AI-generated output may contain elements that are similar to existing works, may be inaccurate about ownership and may not qualify for copyright protection in every jurisdiction.

The Howling Whispers does not guarantee that generated output is free of third-party rights.

Users are responsible for reviewing material before publishing or commercially exploiting it.

## 5. Third-party characters and franchises

Users who create or import content based on third-party franchises, characters, settings, music, images, trademarks or other protected works are responsible for determining whether their use is lawful.

The presence of an import feature does not grant permission from the rights holder.

## 6. Copyright and rights complaints

Rights holders should be provided with a clear way to identify allegedly infringing content and request review.

A useful complaint should include:

- identification of the protected work or right;
- identification or URL of the disputed material;
- contact information for the complainant;
- an explanation of the claimed infringement; and
- a good-faith statement that the complainant is entitled to make the request.

The Howling Whispers may disable access to disputed content while reviewing a sufficiently specific complaint.

## 7. Counter-notice or appeal

Where appropriate and legally available, the affected user should have a way to explain why the material is authorized, licensed, public domain, otherwise lawful or incorrectly identified.

The service may restore material where a complaint is withdrawn, resolved or found unsupported, unless another legal reason requires removal.

## 8. Repeat infringement

Repeated or deliberate infringement may result in publishing restrictions, content removal, account suspension or termination.

## 9. Trademarks and identity

Users must not use another person's or organization's trademarks, branding or identity in a way that unlawfully misleads users about sponsorship, affiliation or official status.

## 10. Preservation and disputes

When a rights dispute, legal claim or valid legal request exists, The Howling Whispers may preserve relevant records for a reasonable period even if the content is no longer publicly visible.
